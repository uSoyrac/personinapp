# Time Management Strategies for IELTS Reading

IELTS Reading gives you 60 minutes for three passages and 40 questions — and no extra time to transfer answers. Most candidates who miss their target band do not lack comprehension; they simply run out of time. The fix is a deliberate strategy for *where* your minutes go. This guide shows you how to budget time, read efficiently, and avoid the traps that quietly drain your clock.

## Budget your 60 minutes

Treat each passage as a 20-minute block: roughly 3 minutes to skim, 15 minutes for the questions, and 2 minutes to transfer and check. If a single question resists you for more than 90 seconds, mark it, move on, and return later. One hard question is never worth three easy ones.

## Skim first, read closely later

Skimming means reading quickly for the gist: title, first and last sentences of each paragraph, and any words in bold or capitals. The goal is a mental map of *where information lives*, not full understanding. With that map, you can jump straight to the right paragraph when a question appears.

## Scan for specific answers

Once you know a question's keyword — a name, date, or technical term — scan the passage to locate it rather than re-reading from the top. Numbers and proper nouns are the fastest anchors because your eye finds them quickly.

## Use the order of the questions

Most IELTS Reading question types (especially gap-fills and Yes/No/Not Given) follow the order of the passage. That means the answer to question 5 usually sits between the answers to 4 and 6. Use this to narrow your search instead of scanning the whole text each time.

## Handle the hardest question types

- **True / False / Not Given:** "Not Given" means the text neither confirms nor contradicts the statement — do not use outside knowledge.
- **Matching headings:** read the paragraph's topic sentence first; it usually carries the main idea.
- **Gap fill:** check the word limit and copy the exact word from the passage.

## Build reading speed before exam day

Speed comes from exposure. Read English articles daily and, while you read, save unfamiliar words straight into your dictionary so you meet them again in context. The fewer words that stop you mid-passage, the faster you read under pressure.

## Common mistakes

- Reading every passage word-for-word from the start.
- Spending too long on one stubborn question.
- Forgetting that there is no extra transfer time — write answers as you go.
- Letting an unknown word break your momentum instead of reading around it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I read the passage or the questions first?**
Skim the passage briefly for a map, then read a question and scan back. Reading every question first wastes time.

**How many questions can I get wrong for Band 7?**
Roughly 30/40 correct in Academic Reading typically maps to about Band 7, though bands shift slightly per test.

**Is it bad to guess?**
No. There is no penalty for wrong answers, so never leave a blank.
