# The Ultimate TOEFL Speaking Templates (2026)

TOEFL Speaking gives you very little time to think, so a reliable structure frees your attention for ideas and delivery instead of organisation. The templates below are frameworks, not scripts — they tell you *what to say next*, while the content stays yours. Used well, they keep you fluent under pressure; used as rigid memorised blocks, they hurt your score. Here is how to use each one naturally.

## Task 1 — Independent Speaking

You get 15 seconds to prepare and 45 seconds to speak about a personal preference. Use a two-reason structure:

> "I prefer ___ for two main reasons. First, ___. For example, ___. Second, ___, which means ___. For these reasons, I believe ___."

The key is the **example**. A short, specific personal example is what lifts you from a 3 to a 4. Decide your opinion in the first two seconds and spend your prep noting one example, not perfect sentences.

## Tasks 2 and 3 — Integrated (Read, Listen, Speak)

Here you summarise a reading passage and a lecture or conversation. Examiners reward accurate reporting, not opinion:

> "The reading passage explains that ___. The speaker supports/challenges this by ___. The first point is ___. In addition, ___."

Take notes in two columns — reading on the left, listening on the right — so the relationship between them is obvious when you speak.

## Task 4 — Integrated (Listen, Speak)

You summarise an academic lecture, often with two examples of one concept:

> "The lecture discusses ___. To illustrate this, the professor gives two examples. First, ___. Second, ___. Together these show ___."

## Make templates sound natural

The fastest way to sound memorised is to deliver a template in a flat, recited tone. Avoid this by:

- Varying your sentence openings instead of repeating "First… Second…" identically every time.
- Stressing the content words, not the template words.
- Leaving small natural pauses rather than rushing to finish.

## Manage your time and delivery

Pacing matters as much as content. Aim to fill — but not overrun — the full response time. If you finish early, you likely under-developed an example; if you get cut off, you spent too long on the introduction. Record yourself and check that you reach your conclusion comfortably.

## Common mistakes

- Memorising full sentences that do not fit the actual prompt.
- Giving an opinion in integrated tasks (only summarise).
- Speaking too fast to "fit everything in", which hurts clarity.
- Ignoring pronunciation — clear, steady speech beats complex vocabulary.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are templates allowed in TOEFL Speaking?**
Yes. Structure is fine; what scores low is content that is clearly memorised and irrelevant to the prompt.

**How can I practise speaking without a partner?**
Use an AI examiner to get instant feedback on fluency and vocabulary, and record yourself to review pacing.

**How long should each example be?**
One or two sentences. Specific beats long.
